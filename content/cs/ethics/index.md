Before any debate about how to govern AI or handle big data, there is a prior question: is there anyone there to be wronged? These notes start where the ethics actually starts, with the philosophy of mind. They ask what consciousness is, whether it depends on biological material or only on functional organization, and whether the systems built in [[deep-learning-revolution|the deep-learning era]] could ever cross from doing into feeling. The governance and surveillance questions in [[ai-governance|AI governance]] and [[surveillance-and-privacy|surveillance and privacy]] assume an answer to this one. The wing makes that assumption explicit.

- [[consciousness-access-vs-phenomenal|Consciousness: Access vs Phenomenal]] - the functional "available for use" sense versus the felt "what it is like" sense, and why moral status hangs on the second
- [[functionalism-and-multiple-realizability|Functionalism and Multiple Realizability]] - mental states defined by what they do, not what they are made of, and the octopus argument that opens the door to non-biological minds

- [[scientific-theories-of-consciousness|Scientific Theories of Consciousness]] - global workspace, higher-order, and integrated information theory as rival markers for when feeling is present

The live argument over whether today's systems have, or could be built to have, whatever consciousness requires.
