Computing did not arrive all at once. It was assembled over centuries: an idea of mechanical calculation, a theory of what can be computed at all, the electronic machine, the chips that shrank it, the software and languages that gave it voice, the personal computer, the network, and the modern world built on top. The notes below are ordered as that story builds, so you can read straight down to watch the field assemble itself, or jump to any stage.

This section is the civilian arc. Where computing grew out of a military need, the deep dive lives in the sibling cluster [[cs/military-computing/index|Computing and the U.S. Military]], and these notes link across to it.
