Cribbage is the game I keep coming back to at the table. Two hands, a deck, and a board you peg around twice to 121. It rewards a little arithmetic and a lot of repetition, which is exactly why it travels well and why it has the history it does.

> [!note] The submarine pedigree
> Cribbage is not just a card game on a boat, it is a submarine tradition with a documented origin. In April 1943, during a patrol in the Yellow Sea, USS Wahoo's executive officer Dick O'Kane was dealt a perfect hand of 29, the rarest hand in the game, by his skipper Dudley "Mush" Morton. The crew took it as an omen of good luck. That board became the O'Kane cribbage board, and to this day it is handed down to the oldest active fast-attack submarine in the U.S. Pacific Fleet. Sources below.

## How I Play

Quick reference for my own table, not a rulebook.

- Race to 121 (twice around the board). Standard two-hand deal of six, discard two to the crib.
- Pegging first, then the show: fifteens, pairs, runs, flushes, and his nobs.
- Crib alternates with the deal.
